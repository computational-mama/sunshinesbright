I've recently been thinking:

What does it mean to think of Sun Shines Bright as a movement and not as a project. Allowing intersections of practice, research and community. Perhaps by understanding tech, climate and care - we create a space to think about hope!

This week I have been working with students from HEAD Geneve on AI and Climate and Feminist tech. We are exploring where our anxieties with AI lie and where we can find intersection to build some possible works. 

## Day 1 

We talk about AI, various things in the world, general impact and finally how we can think about it differently - talking about feminist and non-western critical frameworks.

On a tldraw page we share things about AI that are exciting and what scares us.

One of the students (Antony) also shared this very nice journalistic video piece from the [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/video/opinion/100000010486246/their-eyes.html).

## Day 2 

We started the day with shuffling our seats and making new friends. As the course is an elective there are mixed group from BA and MA and several exchange students (as this was the only other course offered in English!). 

We spoke about our interests and where they interact with AI and Care. 

{{% figure src="venn-head.png" %}} a venn diagram of AI/Care/"your interest" {{% /figure %}}

And we also installed and tested local language models on our own computers to understand how they work! 

## Day 3

We built out our ideas as prototypes. Some were physical, some digital and some conversational.

## Day 4

Two halves.

- In the first half, we learnt how to fine-tune our own models and host them. I have a written guide if you want to try this yourself: [How to fine-tune a Model]({{< ref "/posts/howtofinetuneamodel" >}}).
- In the second half, we looked for ways to connect the models to our prototypes. We also looked for the gaps, both in what the tech can do and in what our prototypes need.

## Day 5

We tried to settle on a final idea for a bot and presented it to our peers.
